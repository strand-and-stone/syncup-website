Roommates do not need a couple's morning ritual. They need a rule for sound. An alarm that is correct for the person who has a shift can still wreck the person who worked until midnight. Write the rule once. Send this page if you do not want a meeting.

This is household logistics, not relationship advice. If the conflict is shift work inside one household, the longer version is [shift work and the shared alarm](/blog/shift-work-shared-morning-routine).

## The agreement

Copy it into a note both of you can open.

1. **Audible hours:** Phone speakers are fine between ____ and ____. Outside that, use a Watch, headphones you are wearing, or a sound that stays in one room with the door closed.
2. **Placement:** Alarms live on a charger in the sleeper's own room, not in a shared hall or on a bathroom counter.
3. **Snooze cap:** ____ snoozes, then the alarm is done. A twenty-minute stack of full-volume alarms is the problem, not the first ring.
4. **Shared mornings:** We only sync an alarm on days we both opted in, such as a flight or a move-out. Other days, each person owns their cue.
5. **Pause:** A sick day, a night shift, or a guest is a pause. Say so the evening before. No scorekeeping.

Apple's Clock alarms are designed to sound even in Silent mode. That is useful when you mean to wake. It is also why "I'll just leave it on vibrate" is not a plan if the sound is not None. If you need a silent morning for the rest of the apartment, use a Watch tap or a cue that does not use the phone speaker. Confirm the sound setting while you are awake. The cue guide is [alarm cues you can actually notice](/blog/accessible-iphone-alarm-cues).

## When a shared alarm helps

Use one shared time when you are both leaving for the same thing: an early flight, a lease inspection, a cleanup before a subletter. Pair it for that day and turn it off after. A standing sync between roommates who do not share a life is usually too much information. The privacy boundary is the same as for couples: time and on-or-off, not location. See [privacy of a shared alarm](/blog/privacy-of-shared-alarm-schedules).

## When it does not

Do not sync alarms to make a roommate "more disciplined." Do not use their missed challenge as a story for the group chat. If the schedules are opposite, the fair outcome is quieter cues, not a shared 6 a.m.

## FAQ

### Do roommates need the same alarm?

Only on days you both have the same reason to be up. Otherwise keep personal alarms and a quiet-hours rule.

### Can I ask a roommate to stop snoozing?

Yes. A snooze cap is a reasonable house rule because repeated full-volume alarms are a shared cost.

### What if someone sleeps through a vibration?

That person needs a cue they can perceive, in their own room. The rest of the apartment should not be the backup speaker.
