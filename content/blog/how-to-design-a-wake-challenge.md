A wake challenge should be short enough to finish while you are groggy and specific enough that a blind tap does not count. It is a wake step, not a puzzle, a personality test, or a way to catch your partner.

SyncUpAlarm's Partner Sync uses challenges the two of you choose: a memory match, an emoji, a tap pattern, or tiny math. This page is how to pick one you will still accept on a bad morning.

## Design for sleep inertia

Sleep inertia is the groggy stretch after waking. Hilditch and McHill describe it as temporary sleepiness and weaker performance, worse after sleep loss or waking from deeper sleep. A challenge that needs reading a paragraph, solving a real problem, or remembering a new rule will fail for reasons that have nothing to do with effort.

Aim for under 20 seconds. If it regularly takes longer, it is the wrong challenge.

## What works

- **Memory match.** A few cards, same layout every day. Recognition is easier than recall.
- **An agreed emoji.** You both know the target before bed. The task is to find it, not to interpret a joke.
- **A tap pattern.** Three taps you practiced while awake. Change it only on purpose, not every night.
- **Tiny math.** One operation with single digits. Not a word problem.

The shared property is familiarity. You chose it in daylight, and the morning version is the same task.

## What fails

- A riddle, a news quiz, or anything that requires curiosity.
- A photo of the room, a barcode in the kitchen, or a step that forces someone out of bed before they have agreed to that.
- A challenge only one person thinks is funny.
- A harder challenge as punishment for yesterday's snooze.
- Anything that uses the camera as proof of where someone is. That is a different product, and it is the wrong one. See [privacy of a shared alarm](/blog/privacy-of-shared-alarm-schedules).

## Fairness

Both people get a challenge they can finish. If one person is a morning type and the other is not, do not give the night owl the "hard" version. The point is a wake step, not a handicap.

Write the choice into the [shared wake agreement](/blog/shared-wake-agreement): the challenge name, the time limit, and the fallback if it is not cleared. A missed challenge means you use the fallback. It does not mean a lecture.

Snooze research does not ban a grace period. Sundelin, Landry, and Axelsson found that about 30 minutes of snoozing in habitual snoozers was not clearly harmful on the measures they tested. A challenge can sit after one agreed snooze. It should not sit after five.

## How to test it

### Step 1: Pick one challenge while you are both awake

Say the name out loud. If either person groans, pick a different one.

### Step 2: Run it in the afternoon

Use the real phones, the real sound, and the place the phone will sit at night.

### Step 3: Keep it for seven mornings

Change one variable at a time. If you change the challenge and the wake time on the same day, you will not know what failed.

### Step 4: Retire it if it causes a fight

A challenge you resent will get disabled. A boring one you can finish will stay on.

## FAQ

### What is the best wake challenge for couples?

The one you both chose and can finish in seconds. Memory match, a known emoji, a short tap pattern, or single-digit math are the reliable set.

### Should the challenge get harder over time?

No. Familiarity is the point. Harder tasks collide with sleep inertia.

### Does finishing a challenge mean someone is alert?

No. It means they completed a wake step. It is not a fitness-to-drive test.
