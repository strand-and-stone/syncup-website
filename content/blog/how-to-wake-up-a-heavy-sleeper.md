The kindest way to wake a heavy sleeper is a plan you both agreed on the night before: their alarm goes first, you're the backup, and everyone knows when to step in. This guide is for the partner or roommate doing the waking; if you're the heavy sleeper, start with [Alarm for Heavy Sleepers: What Actually Works](/blog/alarm-for-heavy-sleepers).

## Agree on the protocol before bed

- Wake time, and how long after it you'll step in.
- What counts as "up" (feet on the floor, lights on, a text).
- What they want you to do, and what's off-limits (no yanking blankets, no cold water).
- Who changes the plan if the morning shifts.

## The gentle wake sequence (in person)

1. Let their own alarm run first.
2. Turn on a light or open the blinds.
3. Say their name at normal volume, then a little louder.
4. A light touch on the shoulder or arm.
5. Ask a simple question that needs an answer ("Are you up for the 8:00?").
6. Check back in a few minutes if they say "five more minutes."

## How to stop being the full-time alarm clock

You're a backup, not the system. Their alarm, their backup alarm, and their phone across the room do the main work. If you're waking them every day, [revisit the plan together](/blog/shared-wake-agreement) rather than escalating volume.

## Waking someone from another city

A call or text only works if they're already near the surface. A shared alarm plan helps more: both of you see the same wake time, and you know when to check. See [Time zone alarm math for long-distance couples](/blog/long-distance-alarm-time-zones) and [When one of you is traveling: a one-week alarm plan](/blog/one-person-traveling-partner-alarm).

Agree on quiet hours and who is (and isn't) responsible for waking whom. See [A quiet-hours agreement for roommates who do not share a schedule](/blog/roommate-quiet-hours-agreement).

## Talk about it later, not at 7 a.m.

Pick a calm moment, ask what would help, and adjust the protocol. Avoid blame language. If they regularly sleep through everything or feel unusually tired, suggest they talk to a doctor.

## FAQ

### How do you wake up a really heavy sleeper?

Agree on a plan the night before, let their alarm go first, then use light, voice, and a light touch, in that order.

### Why won't my partner wake up to their alarm?

Often it's setup: snoozing on autopilot, muted settings, or a sound they've tuned out. If it happens regularly, they should talk to a doctor.

### Can I wake my partner from another city?

You can check in by call or text, and a shared alarm plan shows you when they're meant to be up. Keep expectations realistic.

### Is it okay to wake someone roughly?

Stick to what you both agreed on. Gentle, predictable steps are easier on both of you than shaking or startling.
