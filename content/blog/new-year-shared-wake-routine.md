A New Year wake routine works better when two people agree on it than when one person white-knuckles it alone. This page walks you through a simple shared plan you can set up on January 1 and adjust every week.

## Why shared wake goals slip by February

- One person changes the alarm and the other never hears about it.
- Weekend times drift, so Monday feels like jet lag.
- Goals are vague ("get up earlier") instead of a set time window.

## Set up your shared morning in 5 steps

- 1. Pick one wake window together, like 6:30 to 6:45, not "early."
- 2. Decide who can change it and how the other person finds out.
- 3. Set both alarms to the same plan, and test them one afternoon with Silent mode and Focus on.
- 4. Agree on a quiet rule if one person gets up first in a shared room.
- 5. Hold a 5-minute check-in every Sunday: keep it, nudge it 15 minutes, or pause it.

## Keep weekends close

Keep weekend wake times close to weekdays (for example, within an hour) so Monday doesn't feel like a reset. Adjust together instead of one person sleeping in alone.

## When to change the plan

Shift changes, travel, a new baby, or time-zone moves all mean you re-agree on the plan, rather than one phone quietly changing.

## FAQ

### What's a realistic New Year wake goal for couples?

One shared window you both can hit five days a week, reviewed weekly.

### Should we wake at the exact same time?

Not necessarily. A shared plan can stagger times; what matters is that both people can see it.
