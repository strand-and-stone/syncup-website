Most shared-alarm fights are not about Tuesday. They are about Saturday, when one person still has the weekday alarm armed and the other thought the deal was off. The fix is two named rules, set on Sunday, left alone until the next Sunday.

## Two schedules, not seven debates

Apple's Clock app can repeat an alarm on chosen days. Use that. A weekday alarm that repeats Monday through Friday, and a weekend plan that is either later or off, is easier to trust than one alarm you edit every Friday night.

Health's sleep schedule is still personal. Apple says Clock alarms are separate from a wake-up alarm tied to a sleep schedule. If one of you uses Sleep Focus on weeknights only, that is a personal layer. The shared plan is the alarm you both agreed to see.

## Write the weekend rule in one sentence

Pick one:

- **Same window, softer cue.** The time stays. The challenge is off. Nobody has to prove they are awake for a free morning.
- **Later window.** A shared 8:30 instead of 6:15, still on both phones, so "later" is a time and not a mood.
- **No shared alarm.** Each person keeps a personal alarm or none. You still have breakfast if you want it. You do not owe a ring.

Put the sentence in the [shared wake agreement](/blog/shared-wake-agreement). "We play it by ear" is how Friday becomes a fight.

Gollwitzer and Sheeran's work on implementation intentions is about specifying when and how ahead of time. "On Saturday the shared alarm is off" is that kind of sentence. "We'll see how tired we are" is not.

## Chronotype still applies on Saturday

A later weekend does not erase a lark and an owl. The person who wakes at 7 can get up. The person who needs the morning can sleep. A shared alarm on Saturday is optional. A moral ranking is not. The longer version is [chronotypes and couples](/blog/chronotypes-couples-sleep-compatibility).

Snooze can be looser on weekends if you agreed to that while awake. It should not become unlimited alarms that wake the other person. The evidence and the household cap are in [the snooze button, according to science](/blog/snooze-button-science).

## Sunday reset

Once a week, look at the next seven days for exceptions: a flight, a shift, a guest, daylight saving. Change the repeating days then. Do not discover on Thursday that Monday's alarm is still set for a holiday.

If a week is irregular, pause the shared plan instead of stacking one-off alarms you will forget. The cases where syncing is the wrong tool are in [when not to sync an alarm](/blog/when-not-to-sync-an-alarm).

## FAQ

### Should couples use the same alarm seven days a week?

Only if both people want that. A Monday-through-Friday repeat plus an explicit weekend rule is easier to keep.

### Can one person sleep in if the other is up?

Yes. A shared alarm is a plan you opted into. It is not a rule that both bodies match.

### What if our days off are not Saturday and Sunday?

Use the days you actually have. A nurse's weekend might be Tuesday and Wednesday. The rule is "name the off days," not "copy a office calendar."
