A shared alarm only works if the phone that holds it is on. A dead battery, an overnight update, or a phone left in a bag is an ordinary failure. The backup should be chosen before it happens, and it should not be "the other person will notice."

## Charge is part of the routine

The night setup is boring and sufficient for most misses:

- Phone on the charger, in the place you tested.
- Low Power Mode is fine. A phone at 4 percent in a coat is not.
- Sound is not None. Apple says that if an alarm only vibrates, the sound is often set to None, and StandBy disables alarm haptics, so a bedside phone needs an audible sound.
- Headphones unplugged from the "buried under the pillow" experiment. Apple says the alarm still plays through the speakers when headphones are connected, but a phone you cannot find is still a missed morning.

Do this check when you brush your teeth. Do not do it at the moment you are trying to fall asleep.

## Three backups, in order

1. **A second device you already wear or own.** An Apple Watch with Push Alerts from iPhone can tap you when that iPhone alarm fires, if the Watch is charged and on your wrist. A Watch in a drawer is not a backup. Details are in [Apple Watch and shared wake routines](/blog/apple-watch-and-shared-wake-routines).
2. **A dumb alarm.** A plug-in clock that does not need an app store. Set it to the same window. It will not sync with your partner, and that is acceptable. Its job is to wake you, not to prove anything.
3. **A human fallback you agreed to.** One message or one call if the challenge is still open after the minutes you named. Not a loop of calls. Not the other sleeper being responsible for your battery.

Write the order into the [shared wake agreement](/blog/shared-wake-agreement). "Text me if I am not up" is step 3, not step 1.

## After a miss

Fix the cause the same day, while you remember it. If the phone died, the fix is the charger placement. If an update rebooted the phone at 2 a.m., note that updates can be scheduled and do not run them on the night before a flight. If the sound failed, rerun the volume check from Apple's alarm article before the next night.

Do not add three extra alarms "to be safe" and leave them forever. Stale alarms are how you get a 5 a.m. ring on a day off.

## What you should not build

- A rule that your partner's phone is the real alarm and yours is optional.
- Location sharing to confirm you are in bed.
- Blame as the review. A dead battery is a setup bug.

## FAQ

### Is a partner's text a good backup alarm?

It is a fair fallback after your own cue fails. It is a poor primary alarm, because it depends on someone else being awake and willing.

### Does Low Power Mode block iPhone alarms?

Do not assume a very low battery will last the night. Put the phone on the charger. Test the sound in the place it will sit.

### Should both people set a backup clock?

Yes, if a missed wake has a real cost, such as a flight or a shift. A shared plan still needs each person to have their own power.
