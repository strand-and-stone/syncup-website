US daylight saving time ends at 2 a.m. local time on Sunday, November 1, 2026, when clocks fall back to 1 a.m. An iPhone set to update automatically changes its own clock, and alarms keep their set times. The night before, check the time zone setting, your alarm list, and any shared alarm.

## When the clocks change in 2026

> During 2026, daylight saving time is in effect from March 8 at 2 a.m. (local time) to November 1 at 2 a.m. (local time).

Clocks go back to 1 a.m.

Not observed in Hawaii, most of Arizona, and several territories.

## What happens to iPhone alarms

An iPhone set to update automatically changes its own clock, and alarms keep their set times. The night before, check the time zone setting, your alarm list, and any shared alarm.

## Easing your sleep into the new time

> "Because of the adverse effects of DST on sleep, the American Academy of Sleep Medicine advises that adults obtain at least 7 hours of sleep per night preceding and following DST changes. Graduated adjustment of sleep and wake times beginning 2-3 days before DST can also help with this transition." — [American Academy of Sleep Medicine](https://aasm.org/advocacy/position-statements/daylight-saving-time-health-advisory/)

This is general information, not medical advice.

## The evening-before checklist

Do this on the night the clocks change, not at the new wake time.

### Step 1: Confirm both phones show the same civil time

Unlock both iPhones next to each other. The lock-screen clock should agree. If you are in different regions, compare against a known city, not against memory.

### Step 2: Open the shared alarm and read the label

A 6:30 alarm should still mean 6:30 in the new local time. Say the time out loud. If one of you works a shift that does not move with the clock, write that exception down. Some jobs stay on the old hour for a day.

### Step 3: Check the calendar event separately

A calendar reminder and a Clock alarm are different objects. Move the event and the alarm in the same pass. The difference is covered in [calendar reminders vs alarms](/blog/calendar-vs-alarm-for-waking-up).

### Step 4: Check the other time zone

If you are long-distance, only one of you may be changing clocks. The overlap moves by an hour even though nobody edited the alarm. Recompute the window once. The method is in [time zone alarm math](/blog/long-distance-alarm-time-zones).

### Step 5: Leave a pause rule

The first morning after the change is a bad time to judge the routine. If someone is an hour early or late, use the pause in the [shared wake agreement](/blog/shared-wake-agreement) and fix the setting that night.

## What not to do

- Do not set a second "DST alarm" an hour off and forget to delete it. You will get two mornings.
- Do not turn Set Automatically off to "hold" the old time unless a job truly requires it, and if you do, put a reminder on the calendar to turn it back on.
- Do not assume a Watch, a bedside clock, and the phone all moved together. Glance at each one.
