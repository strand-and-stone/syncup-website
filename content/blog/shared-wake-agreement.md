Send this page before you argue about the alarm. A shared wake agreement is five decisions, written while both people are awake: the wake window, the snooze rule, the challenge, the fallback, and the pause. Once those exist, two iPhones can run the same plan. Until they exist, another alarm app will not fix the morning.

This is a coordination page, not medical advice and not relationship therapy. If either person has persistent insomnia, breathing pauses, or sleepiness that makes driving unsafe, talk with a qualified clinician.

## Copy this agreement

Fill the blanks together, then send the finished version in Messages.

1. **Wake window:** We aim to be up between ____ and ____ on these days: ____.
2. **Snooze:** Snooze is allowed ____ times on normal days and ____ times on high-stakes days.
3. **Challenge:** The proof-of-awake step is ____ and should take under 20 seconds.
4. **Fallback:** If the challenge is not cleared within ____ minutes, we ____.
5. **Pause:** Either person can pause the shared plan for illness, travel, or a bad night without a guilt lecture. We reset on ____.

That is the whole contract. The rest of this page explains why each line exists and where to put it on iPhone.

## Why a page beats a midnight text

"Wake me at 6:30" fails because the plan has no owner after it is sent. One person forgets. The other changes a Clock alarm. A time zone makes the number ambiguous. The person who wakes first becomes the backup alarm, and a miss starts to feel personal.

A written agreement moves the negotiation out of the groggy minute. Gollwitzer and Sheeran’s meta-analysis of implementation intentions found that spelling out when, where, and how a goal will happen improves follow-through across many domains. For mornings, that sentence is: "When the shared alarm fires, I will clear the challenge we picked and stand up."

Mohr, Cuijpers, and Lehman describe supportive accountability as clear expectations that the person helped define, with a supporter who is trusted rather than controlling. A wake agreement fits that shape only if both people choose the rules. It becomes surveillance if one person imposes them.

## Rule 1: Protect the sleep window

Pick the wake time after you protect sleep, not before. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine and Sleep Research Society recommend that adults sleep 7 or more hours per night on a regular basis to promote optimal health. Needs vary. A shared alarm that repeatedly cuts one person below a realistic night is not a sustainable couple routine.

If you are a morning person and your partner is not, do not treat that as laziness. Chronotype is preferred timing. The practical target is the smallest overlap you can both keep. Read [chronotypes and couples](/blog/chronotypes-couples-sleep-compatibility) before you lock an extreme time.

## Rule 2: Write the snooze rule in daylight

Snooze is where unwritten plans break. One person hears the first alarm as "we are up." The other hears it as "the sequence started." Recent research does not support a cartoon ban. Sundelin, Landry, and Axelsson found that 30 minutes of snoozing in habitual snoozers was not clearly harmful on the measures they tested and could ease the first minutes after waking for that group. Mattingly and colleagues found snoozing was common, especially among younger adults and later chronotypes.

Those findings do not mean five alarms over an hour are kind to the person in the next room. Cap snooze. Use zero snoozes on flight or duty mornings. Details are in [the snooze button, according to science](/blog/snooze-button-science).

## Rule 3: Keep the challenge tiny

Sleep inertia is the groggy transition after waking. Hilditch and McHill describe it as temporary sleepiness and impaired performance that is worse after sleep loss or waking from deeper sleep. A wake challenge should be short enough to finish in that state and active enough that a blind tap does not count.

Good options: a memory match, an agreed emoji, a short tap pattern, or one easy math prompt. Bad options: a puzzle you resent, or a check that only one person chose. Partner Sync in SyncUpAlarm is built for this step after two people sync an alarm. See [Partner Sync](/blog/partner-sync-wake-challenge).

## Rule 4: Name the fallback before you need it

Decide the miss in advance.

- Normal day: one retry, then a single message.
- High-stakes morning: one backup call if the challenge is still open after five minutes.
- Long-distance day: if the call window is missed, send a voice note instead of stacking alarms.
- Shift-work day: vibration or a wearable if the other person is asleep.

The fallback is a courtesy, not a scoreboard.

## Rule 5: Pause is part of the plan

Illness, a night shift, jet lag, and a genuinely short night are pause conditions. Say so in the agreement so a pause does not require a negotiation at 6 a.m. Resume on the day you named, or pick a new window if the week changed.

## Put it on both iPhones

### Step 1: Agree on the five lines above

Do this when you are both awake. Change one variable later, not all five after a bad morning.

### Step 2: Separate personal sleep settings from the shared plan

Use the Health app’s Sleep Schedule for one person’s bedtime and Sleep Focus. Use Clock for a personal backup. Apple’s guide to Clock alarms notes that those alarms are separate from a sleep-schedule wake-up. The shared plan should live in one place both people can see. The map of those surfaces is the [complete iPhone alarms guide](/blog/iphone-alarms-complete-guide).

### Step 3: Set the shared alarm and test it in the daytime

Never discover volume, Focus, or phone placement at the real wake time. Apple says Do Not Disturb and Silent mode do not mute built-in Clock alarm sound, and StandBy turns alarm haptics off, so test the actual sound and the place the phone will sit.

### Step 4: Send this page

The share buttons on this article copy the link, open Messages, or post to X. Sending the agreement is the point. Downloading SyncUpAlarm is the next step only after both people accept the rules.

## What not to add

Do not add public streaks, secret location checks, or a rule you would not want applied to you. Do not use the agreement to diagnose sleep. Do not keep a fantasy 5 a.m. alarm because it looks disciplined.

## FAQ

### What belongs in a shared wake agreement?

A wake window, a snooze cap, a short challenge, a fallback if someone misses it, and a pause rule for illness or travel.

### Should we wake at the exact same minute?

Only when the event is shared, such as a call or a flight. Ordinary days work better as a 10- to 20-minute window.

### Can we use this with SyncUpAlarm?

Yes. Agree on the five lines first, then put the shared alarm and challenge in Partner Sync so both iPhones follow the same plan.

### Is this medical sleep advice?

No. It is a logistics checklist. Clinical sleep problems need a qualified clinician.
