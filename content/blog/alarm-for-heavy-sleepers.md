If you sleep through alarms, the setup usually matters more than the volume: where the phone sits, how many cues you get, and whether anyone else knows you're supposed to be up. This guide covers solo fixes first, then how a partner or shared alarm adds a second cue.

## Why phone alarms get missed

- The phone is close enough to snooze half-asleep, so you turn it off without remembering.
- Focus, Silent mode, a muted volume slider, or a low battery quietly changes what the alarm does. Test it.
- One alarm, one sound, every day: it fades into the background.
- The alarm was changed, or never set, and nobody else knew.

## Solo setups to try first

1. Put the phone across the room so you have to stand up to stop it.
2. Use a sound you don't hear during the day, and change it every few weeks.
3. Set a second alarm a few minutes later as a backup, not as a snooze plan ([When the phone dies: a backup alarm that is not your partner](/blog/backup-alarm-when-the-phone-dies)).
4. Pair sound with vibration, or an Apple Watch haptic if you wear one.
5. Run a quick afternoon test with Silent mode and Focus on, so you know what actually rings.

## Loud vs quiet when you share a room

- If your partner wakes easily, try vibration or a watch haptic for you, and a later, softer alarm for them. See [How to Wake Up Without Waking Your Partner](/blog/wake-up-without-waking-partner).
- Roommates: agree on quiet hours and an alarm volume ceiling up front. See [A quiet-hours agreement for roommates who do not share a schedule](/blog/roommate-quiet-hours-agreement).
- A loud speaker alarm in a shared room is a household decision, not a solo one.

## When to skip shock bands and extreme hardware

Shock wristbands, bed shakers and very loud clocks exist, but they aren't the first move. Skip them if you share a bed or room without agreement, have a heart condition, implanted device, or skin sensitivity (check the product's own warnings), or if you're using them to push through ongoing exhaustion. If you regularly sleep through everything, that's a conversation for a doctor, not a louder gadget.

## A 7-day setup check

Days 1–2: move the phone and test the alarm. Days 3–4: add the backup alarm and a new sound. Days 5–7: add a partner or shared alarm if solo still isn't working, and note what changed.

## FAQ

### What kind of alarm works for heavy sleepers?

There's no single answer. Most people do better with a phone across the room, a backup alarm, an unfamiliar sound, and a second cue like vibration or a partner who knows the plan.

### Why do I sleep through my alarm?

Common setup reasons include snoozing on autopilot, muted or Focus settings, or one sound you've tuned out. If it happens regularly, talk to a doctor.

### Can a partner help me wake up without being my alarm clock?

Yes, if you agree on the plan. See [How to Wake Up a Heavy Sleeper (Kindly, and for Good)](/blog/how-to-wake-up-a-heavy-sleeper) for a partner wake protocol.

### Is snoozing bad?
